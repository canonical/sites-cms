# Blog posts

How to write a blog post, sort it into categories, groups and tags, and
credit its author.

## Writing a post

1. Open **Blog** in the menu on the left, then **Blog posts**.
2. Select **Add blog post**. The new post form opens.
3. Fill in the fields. Only **Title** is required.

| Field | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Title | The headline |
| Excerpt | A short plain-text summary shown on post cards |
| Featured image | Picked from the image library |
| Body | The post itself, made of blocks (see below) |
| Author | Who the post is credited to (see "Authors") |
| Publish date | The date shown on the post. It can be in the past, for example for an older article |
| Featured | Tick to show the post in the featured panels on the websites |
| Categories | Which websites show the post, and what kind of content it is |
| Groups | The product areas the post belongs to, in order. The first group is used for the post's label on the websites |
| Tags | Topics, in order |

The **Promote** tab holds the post's address (slug), made from the title if
you leave it empty, and its SEO title and search description.

4. Select **Preview** to see the post, **Save draft** to keep working on it,
   or **Publish**. To publish later, set a go-live date in the post's
   status panel, then publish: the post goes live at that time.

### The body

Select **+** in the body to add a block. There are three kinds.

| Block | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Rich text | Almost everything: paragraphs, headings, bold, italic, links, lists, quotes, horizontal lines, inline code, superscript, subscript, pictures from the image library and videos (paste a YouTube link with the embed button) |
| Table | A simple table of text. You can mark the first row or column as headings |
| HTML (advanced) | Content that rich text cannot hold: code blocks, buttons, and older posts brought over from WordPress. What you type is published exactly as written, so a mistake can break the page. Ask the web team if you are not sure |

## Finding posts

**Blog posts** lists every post with its status, author and the date it was
last updated. Use the search box to find a post by its title.

Select **Filter** to narrow the list:

| Filter | Shows |
|---|---|
| Status | **Live** posts, or **Draft** posts (posts that are not live, including scheduled ones) |
| Category | Posts in any of the ticked categories |
| Group | Posts in that group |
| Author | Posts by that author |

## Categories, groups and tags

Open **Blog** in the menu, then **Categories**, **Groups** or **Tags**.
Each has a name, a slug and an optional description.

- **Slugs are what the websites use.** A slug is made from the name when
  you first save, and does not change when you rename the term. Change it
  only if you mean to: links and lists on the websites that use the old
  slug stop working.
- Two terms of the same kind cannot share a slug. Names can repeat; for
  example there can be two groups called "Networking" with the slugs
  `networking` and `networking-2`.
- A group can sit inside another group: pick it as the **Parent**. A group
  cannot be inside itself, directly or through other groups.
- Deleting a term removes it from every post that uses it. The posts stay.
  The delete screen says how many times the term is used.

Note that **Blog > Groups** are product areas for posts. They are not the
user groups under **Settings**, which control who can do what.

## Authors

Open **Blog > Authors**. An author is the name a post is credited to. It
does not need a CMS account: "Canonical" is an author too.

Each author has a name, a slug, a job title, a bio, a photo and an email.
Only people who can edit authors see the email.

### Linked accounts

An author can be linked to a CMS account, shown as **Linked account**. The
CMS links them by itself when the author's email matches an account's email
(capital letters do not matter). This happens when the author is saved,
when the account is created and each time the person signs in. One account
can be linked to several authors.

When an author is linked, the account becomes the owner of that author's
posts that had no owner. Posts that already have an owner keep it.

You can also link an author by hand: pick the account in **Linked account**
and save. The CMS never replaces a link that is already there.

**To stop an author being linked again:** clearing **Linked account** is
not enough while the emails still match, because the link comes back the
next time the author is saved or the person signs in. Clear or change the
author's email as well.

The post always shows the author's own name, whatever the linked account
is called.

### Deleting an author

An author who is credited on any post cannot be deleted. The delete screen
says how many times the author is used. Move those posts to another author
first.
